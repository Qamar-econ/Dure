Ermera, Timor-Leste — the coffee highlands

A good harvest is not a good price.

Noor is an ambitious mother growing coffee on the slopes of Letefoho, Timor-Leste. She's capable. But is the market treating her well?

Where she farms

The buyers who pay a fair price are in Dili.

Ermera grows close to half of Timor-Leste's coffee.

98%

of Ermera's coffee is bought by just four traders in the capital (survey of 100 farmers, 2014).

Cristovão, Bogor Agricultural University, 2015

Harvest season

Noor has harvested fine Timor-Leste coffee. She needs cash within three days to pay her daughter's school fees.

Monsoon

Timor-Leste's rainy season is notorious.

Flood.

“Recent heavy rains have damaged many roads and bridges in the country, disrupting people's access to markets.”

Asian Development Bank, Timor-Leste
Landslide.

And no truck from Dili will risk the road.

Unfair pricing

One trader's truck makes it up the hill. He names his price. She can't check it, and there's no one else to ask.

The magic of cooperation

Noor isn't the only one on that road. Her neighbours are carrying coffee baskets too.

Collective bargaining

More farmers, more bargaining power. Only the quantity went up, yet the price got better.

This is cooperation. This is Dure.

58%

of 239 studies found that farmer organisations raised their members' incomes.

Bizikova et al., “A scoping review of the contributions of farmers' organizations to smallholder agriculture”, Nature Food, 2020

How cooperatives work

  1. Bargaining power. One farm takes the price it's given. A full truck names its own.
  2. One route to market. One shipment to where the fair prices are, instead of every family making the trip.
  3. Buying together. Seed, fertiliser and transport cost less, bought for twenty farms at once.
“Farmers who are already marginalized … require additional support before they are able to benefit.”Bizikova et al., Nature Food, 2020

So why isn't she benefiting from the Letefoho coffee cooperative?

The catch

Running a cooperative in Timor-Leste is not easy.

3–4years
Time. From recruiting members to the legal paperwork, with slow administration at every step.
$1,000
Capital. A typical coffee household earns about $250 a year. $1,000 is a lot.
15founders
Structure. Then a cooperative needs an assembly, elections and an audit body.

A cooperative formed under a KOICA agricultural value-chain project, Timor-Leste; Decree-Law No. 16/2004 on cooperatives. Coffee income: The Irish Times, 2013.

And then, power.

Women farmers are easily left out. For a while, it was one village; then the members and the opposition.

  1. One. The cooperative copies the village pecking order.
  2. Two. Joining stops being a choice.
  3. Three. Whoever is left out undercuts prices to sink it.

The benefits are real. Getting there is slow, expensive and exclusive, and it ends in a fight over power.

Introducing Dure

What if the village kept the cooperative's advantages, but dropped the cooperative?

Dure works just like a cooperative. A simple text from any phone is all it takes to help Noor get a fair price.

What stays

  • Pooling the harvest
  • Buyers bidding for the whole lot
  • One shared truck and pickup point
  • A record that earns trust

What goes

  • A legal entity
  • $1,000 in share capital
  • Fifteen founders who must agree
  • A board to run, a leader to fight over

Any phone. No internet. No app. SMS has been around since 1992. Dure works even on an old Nokia, and its central server pairs a small AI with a rules engine, so it runs cheaply and accurately.

9 · Technical walkthrough

Technical walkthrough — 1

AI that understands Tetum.

Large AI models have long left Tetum behind. Dure pairs a small model (Qwen2.5-0.5B) with a rules engine, trained on about 3,000 Tetum and English messages. Dialects and spelling slips are fine. It picks out exactly what it needs.

Qwen is an inexpensive open-source model, strong in Asian languages. Training: up to 2,875 synthetic texts from our generator + 250 real Tetum sentences (Labadain-30k+, CC BY 4.0).

Technical walkthrough — 2

AI that knows coffee.

Coffee is graded on SCA defect criteria. Dure taught a small vision model the ones a photo can show, from 1,200 real bean photos, to sort coffee into three grades. It names the exact grade 79% of the time; the final grade comes from a hand check at pickup.

Criteria: SCA green coffee defect classification. Bean photos: Pre-Roast Coffee Bean Grading Dataset (MIT) and Wikimedia Commons.

Some of our training photos · Wikimedia Commons: H. Ulver, M. C. Wright, F. Quijano (CC BY-SA 4.0); Forest & Kim Starr (CC BY 3.0)

Technical walkthrough — 3

AI that makes the market.

Dure AI estimates a fair price from recent sales, buyers, road and rain. Bidding opens a little under it. Farmers whose ask is above the final price sit out.

In the demo, the opening price is set from pre-accumulated synthetic trade data.

Technical walkthrough — 4

AI that measures trust.

Farm size doesn't matter. Trust builds from the number of trades, punctuality and consistent quality. Force majeure, such as a natural disaster, is never counted. Reputation guides how the market is run and when sanctions apply.

In the demo, reputation is computed from pre-accumulated synthetic trade records.

Technical walkthrough — 5

AI that runs two markets.

Dure covers different buyers in different markets. The blended pool is an open market that builds economies of scale. The fixed pool links part of high-reputation farmers' output to steady buyers.

In the demo, the fixed pool runs on pre-accumulated synthetic records.

10 · From the slope to the ministry

An automated Registry and AI policy suggestions.

A farmer Registry is important and effective, but labour-intensive and expensive to keep. Dure works as a market itself, and every deal leaves a footprint. So the Registry builds itself.

Policy makers and extension officers get accurate, good-quality information on limited resources.

AI is not a silver bullet. Noor knows farming better than Dure, so Dure stays out of how she farms. But when the extension officer visits Noor twice a year, they can make the best of it.

12 · Field problems

What are the real field problems?

The road washes out.

Dure books the truck and the pickup point. It can't fix the road, and doesn't pretend to.

Who sees the records?

Farmers' names and numbers stay with Dure. The ministry's Registry shows pseudonymous IDs and village totals, and only for farmers who said yes.

The power or the server goes down.

The rules engine keeps taking offers by text. The small model rechecks them later, and any change goes back to the farmer to confirm.

Electricity is a real problem in Timor-Leste.

40%
of firms had power cuts in a year, losing 9.4% of sales.World Bank Enterprise Survey, 2015
24 h
outages in Viqueque. Dili's hospital runs on generators each time.Tatoli, 2022–23
36%
of Dili customers never report an outage.TANE survey, 2022

Dure always has a backup plan.

A rules engine reads every text instantly, with no model to load. Only the texts it isn't sure about go to the small model. If the server or the power fails, the rules engine keeps the pool open.
